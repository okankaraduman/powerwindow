Spain’s Electricity and Thermal Social Tariffs: a guide for households

Two connected benefits can help with energy costs: one reduces electricity bills, while the other is an annual payment for thermal energy. Knowing which supplier handles the regulated tariff is the first practical step.

Updated 9 November 2026 · Power Window guide

What is the Bono Social Eléctrico?

It is a discount on PVPC, Spain’s regulated electricity tariff. You must hold the electricity contract for your main home, have contracted power of no more than 10 kW, and meet the vulnerability criteria. Until 31 December 2026, the exceptional discount is 42.5% for vulnerable consumers and 57.5% for severely vulnerable consumers. It applies to the PVPC power charge and electricity use up to set limits, so it is not a flat percentage off every part of an unlimited bill.

Eligibility can depend on household income and composition. Other qualifying routes include a valid large-family certificate, certain minimum pensions, and Ingreso Mínimo Vital. Some special circumstances raise the income threshold. Check MITECO’s official eligibility criteria for your situation.

Who offers PVPC?

The major companies’ free-market brands do not offer PVPC under those names. Separate companies called comercializadoras de referencia supply the regulated tariff. For example:

Endesa → Energía XXI

Energía XXI is Endesa’s regulated-market brand.

Iberdrola → Curenergía

Curenergía is the Iberdrola group’s regulated supplier.

Naturgy → Comercializadora Regulada Gas & Power

Comercializadora Regulada Gas & Power offers PVPC within the Naturgy group.

You do not need to choose a supplier from the same group as your current provider. MITECO publishes the complete list of regulated suppliers and their contact details.

How to apply from a free-market tariff

Contact a regulated supplier and apply for the Bono Social Eléctrico. The application form lets you accept a PVPC contract if you do not already have one. If you qualify, the regulated supplier arranges the new contract and tells your previous supplier about the switch. MITECO says the switch is free if you keep the same contracted power.

Prepare the application form, identity documents, proof of address registration (empadronamiento), and any documents needed to establish household composition or special circumstances. Then check that the discount appears on your bill.

What is the Bono Social Térmico?

It is an annual payment for energy used for heating, hot water or cooking, rather than a bill discount. The amount depends on the home’s climate zone, the recipient’s vulnerability category, and any additional contribution from the autonomous community.

There is no separate application: eligibility is based on receiving the Bono Social Eléctrico on 31 December of the previous year. Spain’s autonomous communities and cities manage the payment. Contact the responsible authority in your region with payment questions. Read more at MITECO.

Sources: MITECO’s benefit overview, eligibility criteria, FAQs and regulated supplier list. Check the current rules before applying.
