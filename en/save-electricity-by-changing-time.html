Does changing the time you use electricity save money?

Yes, if your contract charges different energy prices at different times and you can move the same use to a cheaper period. If you pay one price per kWh all day, changing the hour alone does not lower that energy cost. Start with your bill, not today's price chart.

Published by Power Window · 2 October 2026. Original calculations using hypothetical prices.

Plan a loadCheck today's PVPC

First, find the price on your own contract

On your bill, look for the tariff name and the energy rate in €/kWh. The Spanish regulator's “Entiende tu factura” tool helps identify PVPC, a single rate, or rates by period. Check whether the prices you compare include taxes and other items; compare both hours on the same basis.

One rate all day

At a hypothetical €0.20/kWh at any hour, a 2 kWh use costs €0.40 in energy at 10:00 or 22:00. Savings from changing only the time are €0.

Rates by period

Use the peak, shoulder, and off-peak prices in your contract. If the same 2 kWh move from a hypothetical €0.25/kWh period to a €0.15/kWh period, the energy cost falls from €0.50 to €0.30: €0.20 less for that use.

PVPC regulated tariff

For PVPC, Red Eléctrica publishes hourly energy prices. Compare every hour the appliance runs through: the cheapest starting hour may be followed by a more expensive one.

Calculate one full use, not a monthly promise

Imagine an appliance using 1 kWh in each of two hours. If the rates when it starts now are €0.24/kWh and €0.18/kWh, energy costs €0.42. If both later hours cost €0.14/kWh and €0.16/kWh, it costs €0.30. The illustrative difference is €0.12 for that cycle: (0.24 + 0.18) − (0.14 + 0.16). These are not today's prices or a bill forecast.

Real appliances can draw different amounts of electricity throughout a cycle. For a more exact calculation, multiply the kWh used in each hour by that hour's rate, then add the amounts. If you do not know that hourly pattern, the planner assumes an approximate constant power; compare its estimate with readings from your own equipment when available.

What to do next

If you have one fixed rate

Focus on reducing unnecessary kWh or comparing contracts. The PVPC chart is a market reference, but it is not automatically your price. You can review offer terms in the comparator.

If your rate changes by time

Pick a flexible task such as laundry or home EV charging and note its duration. Compare complete windows using your contract's rates. Keep a safe, convenient schedule; a few cents may not justify changing your routine.

If you want to verify the saving

Record the old and new times, plus the cycle's kWh if your appliance shows them. Calculate both options using the same energy amount. Contracted power, meter rental, and other bill items do not disappear when you move one use.

Sources: CNMC, “Entiende tu factura” for contract types and bill items; Red Eléctrica, PVPC for regulated hourly prices. The example rates and calculations are illustrative and shown above so you can check them. Power Window is independent of suppliers and public authorities.
